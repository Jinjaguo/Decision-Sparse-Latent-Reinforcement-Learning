# EXP24 Codex Prompt — Task-Modular Drawer Consequence Coordination

## Mission

EXP23 independently confirmed that soft force scaling solves Stove safety and recovery, but its uniform route underperformed on Drawer. EXP24 must stop forcing one mechanism across tasks. Freeze a task-modular coordinator: Drawer uses goal-consequence recovery with retract-and-switch, Bowl uses a short stable feedback route, and Stove retains the independently confirmed soft-force diverse portfolio.

## Broad implementation and ablation

For Drawer compare goal→smooth→response, smooth→goal→response→medoid, goal→response→smooth, response-first, medoid-first, task-space retargeted goal retrieval, retract versus no retract, progress-stall versus periodic switching, and 300/320-step budgets. Preserve the exact EXP23 Stove module and a stable Bowl control in every Drawer ablation so differences identify Drawer mechanisms. Audit task dispatch, mode reset, force behavior, route validity, and target-future isolation.

Use EXP23 confirmation outcomes only as development evidence for task-module assignment. Freeze the primary before executing the amended EXP5 same-runtime references (30 successful demos, 120 branches) as a new recovery confirmation cohort. These references were used by older criticality work but have not supplied action-consequence recovery outcomes. The action library remains demos 30–40, so there is no demo overlap.

## Success rule

The frozen task-modular primary must improve safe success by at least 10 points over the 140-step default, recover at least 60% of default-demand branches, capture at least 75% of the same-cohort candidate oracle headroom, not worsen safety stops, and be non-inferior in at least two tasks. If passed, perform full code, leakage, hash, denominator, and robustness audits before declaring success. If failed, write EXP24 evidence and continue to EXP25. Stop only on audited success or after EXP62.
